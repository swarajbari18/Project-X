# Module 5 — Taste as a mechanism (learning record)

> Taste is not a gift; it is a machine with three parts — and the student is already running it. Same contract: Tend the example only.

**The Gap (Ira Glass).** Taste runs ahead of skill: beginners already *see* good from bad; the work doesn't match yet. Only production volume closes it. Reading never closes it.

**The machine — two parts, one fuel:**
1. **Discrimination — the fast call** (good/bad/slop), trained by *curated exposure*. RUI p.250 "decisions you wouldn't have made" = this half, one module early.
2. **Reason — the why**, in mechanics words, trained by *articulation* — every end-of-unit sentence ("expensive because—") was a rep of this muscle.
3. **Production — the fuel.** "Rebuild favorites" (p.252) = fuel as instruction.

**Two guards.** The van Schneider hedge: private references + making, not the feed — the feed trains whatever eye *it* wants; taste needs a protected diet. Two styles, never confused: clarity/shipping (Levels, Lou) vs auteur (van Schneider, Semplice) — judging one by the other's rules is the category error; Module 8's personality choice picks from this menu deliberately.

**Where taste lands (out of one head, into artifacts):** the blacklist (item 9) = discrimination written down · tokens (Module 7) = taste made durable · the 10-screens exercise = articulation practice (assistant feeds good/slop/ambiguous; student says why; assistant holds mechanics beside).

**Tend (example only):** the future blacklist ("never accept a default on…") and tokens ("always the same way") are discrimination and reason written down to survive forgetting. The owner sees neither artifact — only their product: one careful hand that knew what it was doing.

**Echoes:** Gap ↔ p.252. Discrimination-flash ↔ Krug's scan (same faculty, different ages — trained since Module 1 unknowingly). Hedge ↔ attention-hygiene. Two styles ↔ p.20 personality.

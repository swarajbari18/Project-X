## One level up, after round 1

1. **Do we need options at all?** Yes. Without them, S1 to S4 each create a situation (the noise Swaraj named) and pay for every stage, and S5 pays six calls for what Lara does in one breath. With them, effort follows need.
2. **Who chooses the option?** Code, from facts the stage wrote: about the business or not, per ask; what each ask refers to; which held item answers it; the fit. The AI never writes "stop" or "continue" (study 11.2.4). Every option has one condition code can test. When in doubt, the stage runs.
3. **Effort is set by facts, never by a rating.** The path's length follows: business or not; answered from what Tend holds or not; fit settled or not; free text or typed; unknowns that matter; new claims on held fields. None is a model's score (study 10.2.3).
4. **What memory and knowledge changes.** Its package before understanding (meanings, open situations with their waits, configured facts) is what makes the arrival loop cheap and right; its narrowing turns the wider look into a filter; before gathering it maps unknowns to items Tend holds, which lets code pass gathering. It never picks the path, never answers anyone, never says an ask is answered.
5. **Quality.** Each stage gets its own lean context, knows the shape of its output before it starts, and opens on the person, not on the system (the 14 September research). The study's stage constitutions (Part 14) are written per stage of this loop.
6. **The conversation manager has one AI stage: understanding.** Routing, the reply decision and the update decision are code. Swaraj: "Routing, I don't think would be much of a LLM thing."

## One level up, after round 2

1. **The whole AI surface of the product loop is four stages**: understanding the arrival; understanding the situation (only when there is something to place); gathering (only when something must be found); deciding (each round); and writing a message. Beside them, memory and knowledge's AI part, before each of those that runs. Everything else is code.
2. **Every option is a fact test.** Round 2 added four conditions (F16 to F19), all code-testable. None needs judgement at the switch.
3. **The answer ending is the most powerful option, so it is the strictest:** evidence-grade items only (a current claim in the sender's own situation, with no open conflict on its field, or a configured fact read from its own store); the person may be told it; the arrival brings no new claim. Learned knowledge never qualifies.
4. **The reply ending is the most exposed, so it is the narrowest:** no claim about the business; at most a repeat of a question Tend already asked this person, and one true line that the rest of the message is being worked on.
5. **Cost before the first reply** (stages + memory and knowledge's AI part): "hi" 2 + 2; a thank-you 1 + 1, no reply; an answer from what Tend holds 2 + 2; Swaraj's refund question with a stale claim 4 + 4; Arjun's Diwali message 4 + 4; research briefs grow with the work, bounded by limits and the progress rule. The 29 October design took 10 to 23 for every business message.
6. **What stays with Swaraj:** answering "any update?" for every open situation at once (H6) would change decided rule B3; reported, not applied.

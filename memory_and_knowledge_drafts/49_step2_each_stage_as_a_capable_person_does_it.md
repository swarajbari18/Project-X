# Step 2: each stage, as a capable person does it

29 October 2026. Swaraj: "just think you are the conversation manager, a human." Before any engineering, this file walks the work as a capable employee does it: Lara at the shop's front desk, who answers WhatsApp, and Kabir, who works the tickets behind her. Then it reads off what the stages are, and what each one may decide.

## The front desk (the conversation manager's work)

**Receiving.** Lara sees who wrote: Arjun, a known customer, or a number she has never seen. She reads the whole message, and the last few messages above it.

**Understanding.** She asks herself, in this order:

1. **Is this about our business at all?**
   - "hi": a greeting. She says hi back, warmly, and waits. No ticket.
   - "what's the capital of India?": nothing to do with the shop. She answers in one line, or, if the shop does not want her chatting, says politely she is here for the shop. No ticket.
   - "what is 17 times 23?": the same.
   - "hi, where is my parcel?": the "hi" is just how he opens. This is shop work.
2. **What does he want, and what is he talking about?** "the status of my refund on that Diwali sale product at airport". She knows the Diwali sale ran 20 to 24 October, and the shop has a store at the airport. If she did not know "Diwali sale", she would look it up in the shop's notes before doing anything else. If nobody had ever written it down, she would mark it as unknown, and not guess.
3. **Do I already know the answer?** She told him an hour ago that his refund was approved and will reach his bank in 3 to 5 days; nothing has changed since. She just tells him again. She does not open a new ticket, and she does not ask Kabir.
4. **Which of his matters is this about?** She looks at his open tickets. One is a refund on a sale-week purchase from the airport store: that is it. If it named an old purchase, she would look back through his past tickets from the sale dates, not through everything he ever bought. If two tickets could fit, she asks him which. If nothing fits, it is new.
5. **Is there anything to do at all?** "thanks!" after his refund closed: nothing to do. Maybe "you're welcome". No ticket. But "thanks, got it" when Kabir is waiting for him to confirm a delivery: that is the confirmation, and it goes on the ticket.

**Routing.** Once she understood, filing is mechanical: she staples the message to the ticket, or opens a new one with her note on top, or two tickets if he asked about two separate things, or holds it and asks him which one.

**What she never does at the front desk:** call the courier, check the order system, or promise anything about the outcome. That is the ticket work. She works only with what the shop already knows and has written down.

## The ticket (the situation worker's work)

**Understanding.** Kabir picks up the ticket. What is new on it since he last looked? What is asked, what is known, what is missing? If the new thing is a typed status from the courier, he just notes it. If it is a long email or a document, he reads it properly.

**Gathering.** What do I need to know before I can act? Where does it live? In what order? He looks only for what could change what he does next. Arjun's sale orders: the order system, between 20 and 24 October. If the shop's notes already hold the answer (opening hours), he looks nowhere else.

**Weighing.** Do the sources agree? The courier says delivered; Arjun says not. He does not pick one; he notes both, and what the shop's rule says to do.

**Deciding.** Is it enough to act? What is the next step: reply, ask the customer, ask the warehouse, update an address, refund, wait, hand it to the manager? He checks his work against what was first asked.

**Acting.** He does it, following the shop's rules: a refund above the limit needs the manager's yes. He writes what he is waiting for, and checks back.

## What the walk shows

1. **The same stages every time; very different effort.** "hi" takes Lara two seconds and never reaches Kabir. A repeat question takes ten seconds at the front desk. The Diwali delivery takes ten minutes spread over a day. A research brief takes hours of gathering. Nobody runs a fixed checklist for everything; each stage does what the message needs, and some stages have nothing to do.
2. **The shop's rules never bend.** Effort varies; the refund limit, the manager's approval and the courier's word as a claim do not.
3. **Most of the front desk is understanding.** Filing is a few seconds once she knows what the message is about.
4. **Understanding uses only what the shop already holds.** Asking the courier is ticket work.
5. **Every stage can end the work.** The front desk ends it for a greeting, a general question, a repeat question she can answer, and a thank-you with nothing to do. Ticket work ends when the decision says "wait" or "done".
6. **A stage with nothing to do is passed, not performed.** No courier call when the answer is already known. No weighing when nothing new came in.
7. **The answer comes from what the shop knows, not from Lara's memory alone.** The shop's notes (memory and knowledge) are what let her understand "Diwali sale" and "airport".

These seven points are the requirements Step 3 must meet.

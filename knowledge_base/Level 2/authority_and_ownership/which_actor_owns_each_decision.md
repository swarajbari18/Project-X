# Which actor owns each decision?

## The short answer

The word "decision" is a trap, and we already split it in the Decision Making category. It has to be split again here.

## Our answer

"Owning a decision" has no single answer until we say which of two very different things we mean:

**1. The business outcome decision.** The business owns its goals, its policies and its final decisions. Tend never decides what the business should want. This is settled — the business remains accountable.

**2. Tend's decision about its own next behaviour** (gather, ask, wait, communicate, escalate, invoke a capability, stop safely). This is selected by Tend, but inside the rules and grants the business set. Tend does not own this decision as a principal; it proposes it and executes only what is granted.

And on top of that, a single "decision" actually involves several distinct parties, which our Human Collaboration work taught us to keep apart:

- **who decides** — chooses the intended outcome or behaviour;
- **who approves / authorizes** — allows it to proceed;
- **who acts** — performs it;
- **who answers** — is accountable for the result.

These four can be four different people (or a person and Tend). So the answer to "which actor owns each decision" is: **it depends on which of the four parts you mean.** The business owns the deciding and the accountability. The grant-holder approves. Tend may act. Accountability resolves to the grantor.

## Why this matters

If we answer "the business owns every decision," we hide the fact that Tend genuinely proposes behaviour and can act autonomously inside a grant. If we answer "Tend owns its decisions," we let it escape the accountability that belongs to the business. Splitting the four keeps both truths.

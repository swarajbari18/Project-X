# Who remains accountable after an automated action, and how do we prevent responsibility from becoming unclear?

## The short answer

For an automated action, the accountable person is the grantor — the person who configured the range the action ran inside. The business as a whole remains accountable for root cause, learning and improvement, in a no-blame way.

## Our answer

We must keep two different meanings of "accountable" separate, because they answer two different questions:

**1. Who answers for the moving work item (the concrete target)?**
This is the person, role, or queue that the situation keeps being pushed toward while work is live. For an automated action that runs inside a grant, this is the **configurator who granted the range.** They are the approver at grant time, so they are the person who answers for the autonomous actions taken inside it. Traceability records which grant was used, when, who set it, and why — so the business can always say who opened the range.

**2. Who measures and learns from failures (the institution)?**
The business as a whole, and no single person is blamed. It finds the root cause — what rule changed, who changed it, why — and improves. This is the no-blame loop. It is not a contradiction with #1; it is a different act.

## Preventing responsibility from becoming unclear

Responsibility becomes unclear when the performer, approver and accountable person are not distinguished. We keep them separate:

- **performer** — Tend, for an automated action;
- **approver** — the grant-holder or the one-off approver;
- **accountable** — the grantor for in-range action, or a concrete person for out-of-range.

No action is ever "Tend's alone," and no action is ever "no one's." Every action traces to a person who approved the grant underneath it.

## Traceability

Traceability is what makes both halves possible:

- It records which grant was in force, who changed it, when, and why.
- It lets the no-blame root-cause loop reconstruct what happened without guessing.

Without traceability, "the institution finds the root cause" is empty. With it, the business can always walk from an action back to the grant and the person who set it.

## What this resolves

This closes the earlier open thread: "what is accountable for an automated action that no human touched?" The answer is: a human did touch it — the configurator touched it when they granted the range. That grant is the approval and the accountability anchor.

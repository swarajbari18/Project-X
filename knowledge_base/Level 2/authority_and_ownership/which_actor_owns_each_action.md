# Which actor owns each action?

## The short answer

Like a decision, an action is not owned by a single actor. It has a performer, an authorizer and an accountable party, and they can differ.

## Our answer

An action has three separable parts, exactly as our Human Collaboration work distinguished for work items:

- **Performer** — who actually carries it out. For an automated action, this is Tend.
- **Authorizer / approver** — who allowed it to happen. For an in-range action, this is the grant-holder (the configurator who granted the range). For an out-of-range action, it is the person who approves that specific case.
- **Accountable party** — who answers for the result. For an autonomous in-range action, this is the grantor who configured the range. The business as a whole remains accountable for root cause and improvement.

## So who "owns" an action?

The word "owns" is too blunt here. Precisely:

- Tend performs the action (when automated).
- A person approved it — at grant time for in-range actions, or at execution time for out-of-range ones.
- A person is accountable for it — the grantor for composed in-range actions, a concrete human target out of range.

There is no action that is "Tend's alone." Every action bottoms out at a person who approved the grant underneath it.

## Why this matters

If we said "the perpetrator owns the action," we'd let Tend (or the configurator) off the hook, or wrongly place blame. If we said "the business owns every action," we'd lose the concrete person who approves and answers for a specific automated step. Separating performer / approver / accountable keeps traceability and the no-blame root-cause loop working.

# How should Tend hold the stakeholders who want to know how the business is doing?

## The short answer

Stakeholders are **not a fourth lifecycle.** They are held as ordinary situations (their asks ride the same understand → gather → decide → act/escalate → explain/trace machinery), with a relationship-type tag derived on the person, and the owner's view shows them as an **obligations/asks ribbon** with deadlines.

## Who we mean by "stakeholder"

From the breadth research (see [`../../research/research_owner_stakeholder_journeys.md`](../../research/research_owner_stakeholder_journeys.md)): a supplier, a landlord, a regulator or tax authority, an investor, a journalist or chamber, a partner, a helper/family member. These people are not prospects and not customers. They do not want to buy; they want the business to give them something — truth, a document, a status, a compliance reply — usually with low friction and often by a deadline.

## How they are held

- **As situations.** A supplier asking for a payment confirmation is an operational problem with a resolution path, exactly like a customer's "where is my order." It routes, it gathers, it can wait, it can escalate, it is traceable.
- **With a relationship-type tag on the person.** The person is an identity anchor; the tag says "supplier," "landlord," "regulator," etc. In v1 the tag is minimal — enough to group and to filter the owner's view — but it is not a lifecycle.
- **As an obligations/asks ribbon in the owner view.** Because stakeholders do not have a stage (there is no "prospect supplier that becomes a customer"), their "journey" is the set of open asks and their deadlines. This is a *projection* over their situations, derived like the commercial journey.

## The auto-vs-owner split (from research, provisional)

Different stakeholder types have different obvious candidates:

- **Supplier/vendor, landlord/property**: auto-handle routine status and standard certificates (COI, payment confirmations); escalate disputes, negotiations, unusual requests.
- **Tax/regulator/inspector**: auto-acknowledge receipt and surface deadline + required docs; escalate almost everything substantive (stakes are high, the owner or accountant decides).
- **Media/community**: auto-log and draft light responses; escalate anything shaping the public narrative.
- **Family/helper**: give controlled visibility and routine pulls; keep decision authority with the owner.

These are provisional. The final split is business configuration and, for high-stakes classes (tax, compliance), leans to the owner by default.

## Why this is v1-safe

Holding stakeholders as situations + a tag + an obligations ribbon avoids building four extra mini-products (a "supplier portal," a "landlord journey," a "regulator journey," ...). It reuses the machinery that already exists, and it gives the owner the relief the research shows they want (nothing silently stuck, deadlines surfaced) without inventing new journeys before real demand exists.

# The meeting as a wait — the spine bridge into Coordination and Time

## Why this document exists

Coordination and Time leave a strand pointing at this category: "how a scheduled meeting or appointment maps onto the wait model … connects to the future Meetings and Human Work category." This document provides that bridge. It is written once here and referenced from the meeting question documents, so two categories never hold two different definitions of the same concept.

## A meeting is a wait

A meeting is not a new shape with its own machinery. It is a case of the thing Coordination and Time already define — a wait:

```text
a decision ("invite a person")            → the decision to create the wait
    ↓
a wait whose subject is people and a date  → subject = the attendee(s); the meeting
    ↓
resume trigger = confirmation then the date arriving
    ↓
on no-show / cancel / reschedule, the wait ends by a failed resume trigger → a meeting event
    ↓
the meeting happens → outcome → the next step is human work
```

## The wait spine, applied

The canonical wait spine (Coordination and Time) names: subject, reason, timing class, resume trigger, release policy, escalation path, visibility. The meeting fills it in:

- **subject** — the people (each attendee) and the date/time;
- **reason** — the purpose or meeting type (sales, support, onboarding, partner contact);
- **timing class** — wait-for-a-date plus wait-on-an-actor (they must confirm, then the date arrives);
- **resume trigger** — confirmation given, then the scheduled moment arrives;
- **release policy** — whether the customer is released with an interim note if the person is not ready, reusing the response-promise / resolution-promise split;
- **escalation path** — the two counters: story (re-express intent) and person (escalate to a senior), then terminal;
- **visibility** — a booked meeting is visible state with its date, per the explainability baseline.

## Failure is a failed resume trigger, not a new concept

A cancellation, a miss, or a reschedule is the wait ending without the meeting happening. Each records a **meeting event**. There is no separate "meeting deletion" concept — it is the wait resolving as failed, which Feeding and Time already know how to represent. What is new to this category is only what the meeting event drives (the two counters), which lives here.

## What this bridge does not decide

It does not re-define the wait spine; it consumes it. Concrete wait mechanics, timers, release-policy values, and check-ins stay with Coordination, Time, and Level 3.

## Working decision

A meeting is a wait whose subject is one or more people and a date, on the shared Coordination/Time spine. This category owns only how the wait's subject, escalation (the two counters) and outcome are specialised for a human meeting — never a parallel model of calendars or appointments.

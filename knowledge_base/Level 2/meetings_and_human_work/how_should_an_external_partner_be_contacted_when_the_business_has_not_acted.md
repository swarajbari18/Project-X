# How should an external partner be contacted when the business has not acted?

## Where this question came from

Level 1 asks how Tend contacts an external partner — such as a delivery company — when the business itself has not acted. Product Vision describes the concrete case: a delivery goes wrong, the business must resolve it, and the resolution often involves people outside the business. Tend prepares the full trail so the partner can act. But the partner only sees what it needs (e.g. tracking), never the customer's private details, and what the partner may see is a rule the business controls.

## Reuse, then the meeting-shape of the contact

Human Collaboration already lists "contact an external partner" as one effect of escalation, and it already scopes the partner's information to what the partner needs. This question makes the contact concrete and reuses the same availability-and-intent shape we use for any meeting.

## When the business has not acted

The trigger is escalation. If an employee who owns the work does not act past the configured rules, Tend may, per business policy: remind, escalate, wait for a defined event, or contact the partner directly. Contacting the partner is one escalation option — it is not an automatic choice. The model of escalation stands.

## Arranging the partner contact

A partner contact is itself a meeting event on the same spine: a wait whose subject is a person and a date. So it uses the same pieces:

- **Eligibility and intent** — the partner has its own people, its own rules, its own availability. Tend gathers the partner representative's intent the same way it would any participant's.
- **Scoped information** — the partner sees only what the business allows: the tracking number, the problem, the requested action. Never the customer's private record beyond the rule. How much the partner may see is business configuration enforced by Tend.
- **The trail** — Tend prepares the full chronological record so the partner can act with context, and the partner owns its part of the work. Tend does not pretend to hold the partner's answers.

## The boundary

The partner is a separate organisation with its own responsibilities. Tend coordinates the communication that gets the partner to act; it does not perform the partner's work, and it only shares what the business has approved. The customer's information stays behind a business-controlled visibility rule.

## What this question does not settle

- The exact data-scoping matrix per partner type and per disclosure tier (Channels & Permissions, Compliance & Security).
- The concrete way through a partner's system (Level 3).

## Working decision

When the business has not acted, contacting the external partner is one business-rule-triggered escalation option. The contact uses the same availability-and-intent meeting shape, carries the chronological trail and only the business-approved information the partner needs, and leaves the partner's part of the work with the partner. The customer's private details stay behind a business-controlled visibility rule.

# How should Tend communicate conflicting information?

## Our current answer

When information conflicts, Tend should preserve the competing claims and communicate the conflict neutrally when it affects the recipient's understanding or next action.

The communication should explain, at the appropriate level:

- what one source says;
- what another actor says;
- what remains unresolved;
- what consequence the conflict has; and
- what Tend or the business is doing next.

## What Tend should not do

Tend should not:

- silently choose a source because it arrived last;
- accuse an actor of lying;
- convert one claim into universal truth;
- expose confidential source details unnecessarily;
- ask a customer to resolve an internal business conflict; or
- perform a consequential action when the conflict blocks that action.

## Different recipients

A customer may need a short neutral explanation and the next step.

An employee or owner may need the complete evidence context and responsibility.

An external partner may need only the conflicting operational details relevant to its investigation.

## Boundary with Trust and Evidence

Trust and Evidence identifies and evaluates the conflict according to source authority and domain rules.

Decision Making determines what Tend should do because of the conflict.

Communication expresses the resulting safe and permitted message.

## Working decision

Tend communicates conflicts neutrally and proportionately when they affect the recipient's understanding or action. It does not hide the conflict or resolve it through wording.

## What this question does not settle

It does not decide which source wins, whether the conflict blocks all work, or who must investigate it.

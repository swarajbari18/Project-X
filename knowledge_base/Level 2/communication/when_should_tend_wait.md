# When should Tend wait?

## Our current answer

Tend should wait when communication would be premature, misleading, unnecessary, or unsafe.

This may happen when:

- a material fact is missing;
- a material ambiguity remains unresolved;
- conflicting information affects the possible response;
- approval or human responsibility is pending;
- the next meaningful event has not happened;
- the situation has not changed since the previous communication; or
- the business has not yet decided what it wants to communicate.

Waiting must not mean forgetting.

The situation should retain why it is waiting, what it is waiting for, who or what is expected to respond, and what should happen when the wait ends.

## Different kinds of waiting

The conceptual meaning of waiting may be:

- silent waiting because no outward message is useful;
- acknowledged waiting because the actor should know that work is active;
- waiting for information;
- waiting for human responsibility;
- waiting for a business decision; or
- waiting for a change in the situation.

These meanings should not be collapsed into one generic state.

## Boundary with Coordination and Time

Decision Making chooses to wait. Coordination and Time manage the resulting waiting responsibility and state.

Communication decides whether that wait itself needs to be expressed to an actor.

## Working decision

Tend waits when communicating now would be less safe or less useful than waiting, while keeping the reason and next condition visible.

## What this question does not settle

It does not define timers, reminders, escalation thresholds, channel windows, or technical scheduling.

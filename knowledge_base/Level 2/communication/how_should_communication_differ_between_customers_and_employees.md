# How should communication differ between customers and employees?

## The question is broader than two audiences

The original wording contrasts customers and employees. The product context now includes prospects, owners, administrators, external partners, and other interested parties.

The deeper question is:

> How should communication differ according to an actor's relationship, responsibility, authority, and information needs?

## Customers and prospects

Communication to a customer or prospect should prioritize:

- clear language;
- the answer or current state;
- the next useful step;
- honest uncertainty;
- realistic expectations; and
- continuity with what the business previously communicated.

It should not expose internal blame, private information, unsupported assumptions, or unnecessary operational detail.

## Employees and owners

Communication to an employee or owner should prioritize:

- the relevant situation;
- evidence and unresolved claims;
- what is missing;
- the responsibility being requested;
- urgency and deadlines;
- the authority required; and
- what has already been attempted.

## External partners

Communication to a partner should explain the part of the operational problem that the partner owns, the action or investigation requested, and the information needed to perform it.

It should not expose the full customer or business record.

## Working decision

Communication should be role-specific, not merely audience-specific. The same situation may require different messages to different actors because their responsibilities and permissible context differ.

## What this question does not settle

It does not define the complete actor or authority model. Those remain responsibilities of Level 1 framing, Human Collaboration, and Authority and Ownership.

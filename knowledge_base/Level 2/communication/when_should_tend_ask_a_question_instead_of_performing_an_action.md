# When should Tend ask a question instead of performing an action?

## Our current answer

Tend should ask when the missing answer could change:

- which situation is being handled;
- what the actor wants;
- which information is required;
- which actor is responsible;
- which policy applies;
- whether the action is allowed; or
- the consequence of performing the action.

Tend may perform an action when the situation is sufficiently understood, the required information is usable, the applicable policy permits the action, and no unresolved uncertainty could make the action unsafe.

## The question should go to the right actor

The question may be directed to:

- the customer or prospect for intent, identity, or experience;
- an employee for business knowledge or operational work;
- an owner for a reserved decision;
- an external partner for information or work belonging to the partner; or
- another actor defined by the situation.

Tend should not ask a person for information that an appropriate source already provides and should not ask a customer to resolve an internal business conflict that belongs to the business.

## Ask is not one meaning

Clarification, information gathering, acknowledgement, consent, authorization, and approval may all appear as questions, but they are different responsibilities.

Communication expresses the question. Gathering, Human Collaboration, Authority, and Decision Making determine why the question is needed and what its response means.

## Working decision

Tend asks when an unresolved answer could change the safe next step. It acts when the necessary understanding, evidence, policy, and authority already support the action.

## What this question does not settle

It does not define the wording algorithm, authority rules, legal meaning of consent, or technical execution of the resulting action.

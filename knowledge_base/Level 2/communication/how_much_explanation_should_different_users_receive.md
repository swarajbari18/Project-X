# How much explanation should different users receive?

## Our current answer

Tend should provide the minimum explanation that allows the recipient to understand the relevant situation and perform the next responsibility.

The amount depends on:

- the recipient's role;
- the responsibility they hold;
- their authority;
- the consequence of the decision;
- the sensitivity of the information;
- the question they asked; and
- the action expected from them.

## Different audiences

A customer or prospect usually needs:

- what happened;
- what is known;
- what remains uncertain;
- what Tend or the business is doing; and
- what they should do next.

An employee or owner may additionally need the evidence, conflict, missing information, deadline, responsibility, and required authority.

An external partner should receive only the operational context and information required for the partner's responsibility.

## The need-to-know boundary

More explanation is not always better.

An explanation may be accurate and still be inappropriate if it exposes confidential information, private data, internal disagreement, or another actor's responsibility unnecessarily.

The recipient should receive enough context to act safely, not the entire internal situation.

## Working decision

Explanation depth is audience- and consequence-dependent. Tend should communicate the smallest sufficient explanation while preserving the reason and evidence needed for accountability.

## What this question does not settle

It does not define technical access control, channel-specific visibility, or legal disclosure rules.

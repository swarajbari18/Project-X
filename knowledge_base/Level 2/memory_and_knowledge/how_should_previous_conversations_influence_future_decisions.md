# How should previous conversations influence future decisions?

## Status

Answered — working decision, pending review.

## Previous conversations do not automatically influence every situation

Tend should carry a previous conversation forward only when it is relevant to the current situation and permitted by scope and access rules.

A closed situation should not silently become context for a new situation merely because the same customer or employee is involved.

## There are several kinds of influence

Previous conversations may provide:

- a direct current claim;
- a reference to an earlier situation;
- a local vocabulary meaning;
- an unresolved question;
- a previous failed attempt;
- a reusable procedural lesson;
- a human preference or communication convention; or
- evidence that a current interpretation may be wrong.

These should not all be inserted into context in the same way.

## Influence follows the current decision need

If Tend is trying to understand an employee’s wording, local vocabulary and previous interpretations may be useful.

If Tend is gathering information, previous tool failures and source-selection lessons may be useful.

If Tend is deciding whether to invoke a capability, relevant preconditions and failure warnings may be useful.

If Tend is communicating, the current situation and applicable communication preferences may be useful.

Previous conversations should not be retrieved just because their words look similar.

## Current evidence wins over historical memory

Previous experience can guide what Tend checks.

It cannot replace current business-system information, current authority or current policy.

If old memory conflicts with current authoritative information, the old memory becomes stale, conflicting or irrelevant for this decision.

## Working decision

Previous conversations influence future behaviour only through explicit relevance, scope, currentness, evidence and context selection.

They may guide understanding, gathering, planning or communication, but they do not silently determine a business outcome or override current authority.

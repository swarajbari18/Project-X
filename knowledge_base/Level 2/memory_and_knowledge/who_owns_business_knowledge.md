# Who owns business knowledge?

## Status

Answered — working decision, pending review.

## Ownership has several meanings

The earlier Trust and Evidence work separated:

- source;
- owner;
- authority;
- truth;
- permission; and
- experience.

The same separation is required here.

## The business owns official knowledge

The business owns:

- its rules;
- its official definitions;
- its policies;
- its approvals;
- its operating responsibilities;
- its source records; and
- the accountability for its decisions.

Tend may represent this knowledge for use, but Tend does not own the meaning of the business.

## Tend owns its experience of operating

Tend may own or maintain the record of:

- what it attempted;
- what information it used;
- which tool it selected;
- what failed;
- what correction it received; and
- what learning item it proposed.

That is Tend’s operational experience, not the business’s official policy.

## Who may correct learned memory?

The business should be able to correct, reject, approve, retire or restrict learned knowledge according to its configuration and roles.

Automatic learning may create active guidance by default, but the business must remain able to inspect and change what Tend carries forward.

The exact human roles belong to the Authority and Ownership and Human Collaboration categories, but the basic boundary is already clear:

> Tend can maintain its experience. The business remains responsible for official business knowledge.

## Working decision

Ownership of the underlying business truth remains with the appropriate business actor or system.

Tend owns the coordination and trace of its own operation, and may maintain derived learning for future behaviour without becoming the owner of the business rule or record.

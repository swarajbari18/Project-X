# What should Tend remember?

## Status

Answered — working decision, pending review.

## The problem

If Tend remembers everything, future context becomes noisy, stale and difficult to trust.

If Tend remembers too little, it repeats the same mistakes and makes the business repeat itself.

The question is therefore not:

> How do we store all conversations?

It is:

> What can change Tend’s future behaviour in a useful, traceable and business-scoped way?

## Tend should remember several different things

### What happened

Tend should retain the experience of the work:

- the situation version;
- the information available at the time;
- the interpretation Tend formed;
- the information it gathered;
- the tools it selected;
- the arguments it used;
- the result of each tool call;
- human intervention;
- customer or business outcome; and
- any later correction.

This is episodic experience. It is the evidence from which learning can be formed.

### What the business means

Tend may remember local vocabulary, aliases, abbreviations, employee language and the meaning of terms in a particular business.

This is not automatically an official glossary.

It is a business-scoped interpretation aid with evidence and status.

### What Tend has learned to do better

Tend may remember procedural guidance such as:

- a tool precondition;
- a source to check first for a particular question;
- a common missing field;
- a known interpretation trap;
- a safe clarification question;
- or a recurring handoff problem.

This guidance remains advisory and cannot override controls.

### What Tend got wrong

Tend should remember its own failure patterns:

- wrong tool selection;
- wrong tool arguments;
- misunderstanding an employee term;
- treating an assumption as a fact;
- failing to detect a conflict;
- asking for information that could not change the next step;
- or communicating before the situation was understood.

The memory should preserve the failure and the correction, not only a short sentence saying “do better next time.”

## What Tend should not treat as the same thing

Conversation history, current situation state, business knowledge, experience, procedural guidance and policy are different objects.

A conversation may remain history without becoming knowledge.

An experience may be retained for audit without being retrieved as guidance.

A learned interpretation may be useful without being an official business definition.

## Working decision

Tend remembers information that has future operational value, including experience, local meaning, reusable procedure and its own failure patterns.

Every memory remains connected to its evidence, scope, time, status and allowed influence.

Remembering something does not make it true, official or universally applicable.

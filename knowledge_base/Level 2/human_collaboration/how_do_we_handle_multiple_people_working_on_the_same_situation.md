# How do we handle multiple people working on the same situation?

## Where this question came from

The product vision expects different employees, customers, partners and systems to contribute to one business situation.

The situation model may remain one situation even when several conversations and human work items exist around it.

The problem is that several people can help without all becoming indistinguishable owners.

## Our current answer

Tend should represent one situation as capable of containing several related human work items.

Each work item should make its own responsibility visible.

The situation should preserve:

- the primary accountable owner;
- the person or group performing each task;
- consulted contributors;
- people who must approve something;
- people who only need to be informed;
- the work each actor is responsible for;
- dependencies between work items;
- deadlines and waiting conditions;
- and the information each actor is allowed to see.

This is similar to the distinction made by responsibility and decision frameworks, but it is not a decision to adopt a specific framework.

## One situation can contain several work items

For a delivery dispute, Tend might create:

- an internal investigation for an operations employee;
- a customer confirmation request;
- a partner investigation;
- and an approval request for compensation.

These work items are related, but they do not have the same owner, authority or data scope.

## Preventing duplicate and contradictory work

When a new person becomes involved, Tend should show what work already exists.

The person should be able to see:

- what has already been requested;
- who is responsible;
- what information has arrived;
- what remains unknown;
- what another person is already doing;
- and what action they are expected to take.

The same situation should not silently create several people investigating the same question unless the business wants parallel work.

If parallel work is intentional, Tend should record why it is parallel and how the results will be combined.

## When information changes

If a new claim changes the situation, all affected work must be re-evaluated.

An earlier assignment should not remain active simply because it was created before the new information arrived.

The work may be cancelled, changed, transferred or escalated, while the earlier assignment remains traceable.

## What this question does not settle

It does not decide whether every situation must have exactly one accountable owner.

It does not decide how conflicting human conclusions are resolved.

It does not decide whether a group approval needs one response or every required response.

Those are business and authority rules.

## Working decision

Multiple people may work on one situation, but their roles, work items, authority, data scope and accountability must remain distinguishable.

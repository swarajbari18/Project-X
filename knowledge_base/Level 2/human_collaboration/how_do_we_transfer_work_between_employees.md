# How do we transfer work between employees?

## Where this question came from

The Level 1 framing expects employees to become unavailable, change roles, hand work to one another and escalate unresolved situations.

The first simple interpretation was “change the assignee.”

That is not enough.

A handoff changes responsibility while the situation may still be changing.

## Our current answer

A handoff should explicitly transfer responsibility for a defined piece of work while preserving the context, history, authority, deadlines and customer commitments associated with it.

The handoff should explain:

- why the work is being transferred;
- what the situation currently means;
- what has already happened;
- what information is known, missing or conflicting;
- what action is expected next;
- what deadline or waiting condition applies;
- what authority is required;
- what data the receiving actor may see;
- and what the previous owner has already communicated.

## Notification is not acceptance

Sending a request to another employee does not prove that the employee has accepted responsibility.

Until the handoff is accepted, Tend should keep the handoff visible as pending and preserve the previous owner, fallback owner or escalation path.

The business may define a different rule for a queue or on-call schedule, but that rule must be explicit.

## Handoff versus escalation

A handoff may be routine.

Escalation usually changes urgency, authority, visibility or the level of responsibility.

An escalation may also create a handoff, but the two concepts should not be treated as identical.

## The situation may change during handoff

If new information arrives while work is being transferred, the receiving actor should work from the latest situation version.

The earlier state and earlier owner remain traceable.

The new information may make the handoff unnecessary, change the required action or require a different authority.

## What this question does not settle

It does not decide whether acceptance must be explicit for every kind of work.

It does not define the exact handoff timeout.

It does not decide whether a manager may force a transfer or how customers are informed.

## Working decision

Tend must not treat reassignment or notification as proof that responsibility has been accepted.

A handoff is a visible change in responsibility with a preserved history and a clear fallback if acceptance or completion does not occur.

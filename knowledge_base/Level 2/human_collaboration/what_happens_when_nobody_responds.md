# What happens when nobody responds?

## Where this question came from

The Product Vision identifies business inaction as the hardest problem Tend must solve.

The Level 1 framing says that Tend should make the responsibility and deadline visible, then follow the business’s escalation rules.

The missing part was what escalation actually means.

## Our current answer

When nobody responds, Tend should distinguish what has failed before choosing the next step.

These are different situations:

- the request was not delivered;
- the request was delivered but not acknowledged;
- the request was acknowledged but no work has progressed;
- the person rejected the work;
- the person is unavailable;
- the person completed the work but did not record it;
- or the work became unnecessary because the situation changed.

Tend should never treat silence as completion.

## Escalation can change different things

Depending on business policy, Tend may:

- remind the current owner;
- increase the urgency or priority;
- notify the current owner through another permitted channel;
- notify a supervisor or business owner;
- add a contributor or responder;
- reassign the work to another person, role, team or queue;
- move the work to a higher authority;
- contact an external partner;
- update the customer or stakeholder about the delay;
- wait safely for a defined event;
- or stop safely when no permitted path exists.

These are different effects of escalation.

## Acknowledgement and completion

The escalation clock may stop when someone acknowledges the request, but the work may remain open.

Tend should continue to track completion, waiting, transfer and further escalation separately.

## The escalation path is business-specific

The path may depend on:

- situation consequence;
- customer or account importance;
- urgency and priority;
- business hours;
- service expectations;
- the actor’s role;
- the action’s reversibility;
- the required authority;
- and whether an external partner is involved.

Small businesses may have one owner and one fallback.

Larger organizations may have frontline teams, specialist queues, on-call schedules, managers, executives and partner contacts.

The core problem remains the same, but the ladder becomes deeper through configuration.

## What should travel with an escalation?

An escalation should preserve the chronological trail needed by the next person.

That includes:

- the current problem statement;
- what the customer or stakeholder asked;
- the relevant situation history;
- what information was used;
- what is missing or conflicting;
- who owned the work;
- what was requested;
- when it was requested;
- what response or acknowledgement occurred;
- and how much time or urgency remains.

An external partner should receive only the information permitted for that partner.

## What this question does not settle

It does not define universal timers, reminder counts, priority levels or escalation ladders.

It does not decide whether a customer should always be told about internal inaction.

It does not define when Tend may contact a partner directly.

## Working decision

Silence is an observable failure state, not a successful outcome.

Tend should keep the work visible and follow an explicit, traceable escalation path that may change urgency, visibility, notification, ownership, authority or external routing.

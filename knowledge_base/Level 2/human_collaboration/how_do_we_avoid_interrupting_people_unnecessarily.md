# How do we avoid interrupting people unnecessarily?

## Where this question came from

Tend exists partly because businesses spend too much time asking people for routine updates.

But avoiding unnecessary interruption does not mean avoiding human involvement.

It means asking for human attention when that attention can materially change the safe next step.

## Our current answer

Before interrupting a person, Tend should consider:

- whether the person’s response can change the next safe behaviour;
- how urgent the situation is;
- what happens if Tend waits;
- the consequence of being wrong;
- whether another source can answer safely;
- whether the work is already assigned;
- whether the request can be combined with another request;
- whether the person is currently available;
- whether the channel is appropriate;
- and whether the person has consented to or configured this type of notification.

The relevant question is not simply “Can a human help?”

It is:

> Is human attention necessary now, and is this the right human request to make?

## Different requests have different interruption costs

A customer confirming the scope of a lookup may be a lightweight acknowledgement.

An employee investigating a delivery failure is a larger work request.

An owner approving compensation is a high-consequence interruption.

A partner meeting may require several people and advance coordination.

Tend should not treat all human involvement as the same size of interruption.

## Avoiding interruption without hiding risk

Tend may safely:

- wait for an already expected event;
- retrieve information from an authorised system;
- combine related questions into one request;
- provide a limited answer while a deeper investigation continues;
- notify a queue instead of interrupting several individuals;
- or use a configured low-priority notification.

It should not suppress a human request merely because the request is inconvenient when the situation is consequential or time-sensitive.

## Notification is part of the decision

The actor may have notification preferences, working hours, on-call coverage or channel constraints.

These constraints affect how Tend asks for attention.

They do not automatically remove the underlying responsibility.

If a high-consequence situation cannot wait, Tend may need to use an escalation path even outside normal working hours.

## What this question does not settle

It does not define universal urgency thresholds, notification frequency or business hours.

It does not decide whether a business should batch requests or use a queue.

It does not allow Tend to hide important work merely to reduce interruptions.

## Working decision

Tend should request human attention only when the actor’s participation is relevant to the next safe step, while using consequence, urgency, availability, communication rules and existing work to control the cost of that interruption.

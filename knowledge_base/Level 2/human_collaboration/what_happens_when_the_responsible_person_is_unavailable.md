# What happens when the responsible person is unavailable?

## Where this question came from

The Level 1 framing identifies unavailable actors as a normal failure class.

An employee may be on leave, outside working hours, no longer hold the role, or be unable to access the relevant system.

A partner representative may also be unavailable.

This is different from assigning work to someone who is available but does not respond.

## Our current answer

When Tend knows that the responsible actor is unavailable, it should use the business’s coverage, delegation, fallback or escalation rules rather than pretending the work is progressing.

Possible paths include:

- route to the current on-call person;
- route to a team or queue;
- use a delegated authority;
- assign a backup owner;
- wait until the actor becomes available;
- contact the partner’s fallback contact;
- notify the business owner;
- or stop safely when no permitted substitute exists.

## Availability has several meanings

Tend should distinguish:

- known leave or absence;
- outside working hours;
- unavailable communication channel;
- unavailable business system;
- overloaded or busy;
- role or employment change;
- authority that has expired;
- and unknown availability.

A free calendar does not prove that an employee is genuinely available for a meeting or investigation.

Availability may depend on role, preferences, workload, business rules and the type of work.

## Authority may change with availability

If an employee becomes unavailable, the replacement may be able to perform the work but not approve the same action.

Tend must not transfer authority merely because it transfers work.

The fallback actor must be both able and authorised to take the required responsibility.

## What if there is no substitute?

If only one actor can safely provide the information or make the decision, Tend may need to wait.

The waiting responsibility should remain visible with its reason, expected event, deadline and escalation path.

If waiting would create unacceptable risk and no safe substitute exists, Tend should escalate to the appropriate owner or stop safely.

It should not invent an answer or silently use a less-authorised actor.

## What this question does not settle

It does not define how availability is detected.

It does not decide whether every role needs a backup.

It does not define the business’s leave, shift, on-call or delegation policies.

## Working decision

Known unavailability should cause Tend to evaluate coverage, delegation, fallback and authority before waiting or continuing.

Work and authority must be transferred separately when necessary.

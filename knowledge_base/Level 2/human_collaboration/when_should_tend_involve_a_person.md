# When should Tend involve a person?

## Where this question came from

The Level 1 framing says that some situations require human judgement, approvals or business experience.

The first interpretation was that this question asks when Tend should stop automatic work and create work for an employee.

That is part of the answer, but it is not the whole answer.

Customers, prospects, partners, owners, investors and other stakeholders may also need to participate in a situation.

## Our current answer

Tend should involve a human actor when the next safe behaviour requires that actor to provide information, acknowledge a request, give permission, approve an action, exercise judgement, perform work, represent the business, or accept responsibility.

Human involvement is not triggered only by low model confidence.

It may be required because:

- the needed information exists only with that actor;
- the customer must confirm the scope of a request;
- a business rule requires a person’s approval;
- the action has consequences the business reserves for a human;
- evidence remains materially conflicting;
- policy is missing, contradictory or expired;
- the actor must make an exception;
- the business must be represented by an authorised person;
- an external partner must investigate or perform its own work;
- a stakeholder request needs a special disclosure path; or
- the person has explicitly asked to participate.

The same situation may require more than one kind of human involvement.

For example, a delivery dispute may need:

- the customer to confirm non-receipt;
- an employee to investigate the order;
- a partner representative to investigate the delivery;
- and an owner to approve compensation.

## Human involvement is not one state

Tend should distinguish at least these meanings:

- the actor is the subject of the situation;
- the actor is providing information;
- the actor is confirming or acknowledging a request;
- the actor is authorising access or an action;
- the actor is approving a business decision;
- the actor is performing work;
- the actor is accountable for the work;
- the actor is being consulted; or
- the actor is only being informed.

“Human involved” is therefore too weak to describe what happened.

## Customer acknowledgement

A customer may need to acknowledge that Tend should retrieve or use particular information before a capability is invoked.

That acknowledgement may be a business confirmation or a permission gate.

It should not automatically be called legal consent. Whether consent is required, and what makes it valid, depends on the purpose, relationship, jurisdiction and applicable rules.

Human Collaboration records the request and the response. Authority and business policy determine what the response permits.

## Relationship to Decision Making

Decision Making selects a behaviour such as:

- ask an actor;
- create human work;
- request approval;
- wait for acknowledgement;
- arrange a meeting; or
- escalate.

Human Collaboration determines how the human participation is created and managed.

It does not decide the business’s policy or outcome.

## What this question does not settle

This question does not define a universal list of actions that always require a human.

That boundary depends on consequence, business policy, authority, evidence, market rules and the actor’s role.

It also does not decide the exact legal meaning of a customer’s confirmation.

## Working decision

Human involvement is required when a human actor must contribute information, confirmation, authority, judgement, action, representation or accountability that Tend cannot safely replace or bypass.

# How do we represent ownership of ongoing work?

## Where this question came from

The Product Vision says that Tend must keep work moving until someone safe takes responsibility.

The Level 1 framing also says that different employees may work on the same customer and have different responsibilities.

This means ownership cannot be represented only as “the employee connected to the customer.”

## Our current answer

Ownership should be represented at the level of a human work item.

The work item should make visible:

- what responsibility exists;
- who is currently responsible for performing it;
- who is accountable for its result;
- who may approve or authorize the next action;
- who is contributing or being consulted;
- who needs to be informed;
- when ownership began;
- what deadline or waiting condition applies;
- what data and context the owner may see;
- and what happened when ownership changed.

The business may use one person, a role, a team or a queue as the owner target.

The responsibility must still be unambiguous.

## Ownership is not accountability for the whole business outcome

An employee may own the work of investigating a delivery failure.

The business remains accountable for the customer relationship and its policy.

The delivery partner remains responsible for its delivery operation.

Tend owns coordination and traceability, not the underlying business operation.

## One situation may have several owners

One situation can contain several work items.

The accounts employee may own the refund investigation.

The support employee may own communication with the customer.

The owner may own an approval.

These are different responsibilities.

Tend should not force them into one artificial owner if the business has genuinely separated the work.

However, an unresolved group of people should not be presented as ownership without saying who is accountable for the specific work.

## Ownership changes over time

Ownership changes when work is accepted, transferred, delegated, escalated, completed, cancelled or superseded.

Each change should remain traceable.

If an employee leaves, becomes unavailable or changes role, Tend should not silently retain them as the active owner.

The authority and fallback rules must determine what happens next.

## What this question does not settle

It does not prescribe a specific responsibility framework.

It does not decide whether every business needs a single accountable owner for every work item, although leaving accountability undefined is unsafe.

It does not decide how ownership is displayed to customers, employees or owners.

## Working decision

Ongoing ownership is a time-varying responsibility attached to a defined human work item.

Tend must distinguish the performer, accountable owner, approver, contributor and informed stakeholder, while preserving changes in ownership over time.

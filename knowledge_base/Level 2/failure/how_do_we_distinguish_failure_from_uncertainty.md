# How do we distinguish failure from uncertainty?

## Where this question comes from

This is the boundary that the whole category turns on. If we cannot tell the two apart, we either wait forever or panic-raise over nothing. The two live on the same facts; what differs between them is only whether the current path is still usable.

## Our answer

The distinction is the bound. Uncertainty and failure are not two kinds of mess. They are two moments of the same wait.

> **Uncertainty:** the current path is still usable, so waiting, asking, or investigating further is honest. We are inside the bound, and nothing contradicts the path.
>
> **Failure:** the current path is no longer usable. The bound crossed without resolution. Continuing to wait would go past honest into pretending.

The same observable facts can sit on either side of the line depending on the bound the card carries. A carrier goes silent; after twelve hours we still call it "waiting," because the path may still resolve. When it crosses what the carrier contract promised, we stop calling it a wait and call it a failure: the path "read the carrier" cannot deliver, and the card has to change course.

## Why the distinction has to be visible, not soft

The product vision says a failure must never be hidden, and every important action must be explainable. If the difference between "we are waiting" and "this path is dead" is felt rather than recorded, then no one can see why the card changed course. So the distinction is not an internal feeling; it becomes a recorded event. Inside the bound, waiting. Past the bound, a declared failure with evidence. That record is what makes the next decisions — triage, recovery, escalation — explainable.

## What is decided, what is open

Decided: the line is the bound. Waiting inside the bound is honest; declared failure past it is not an opinion but a recorded fact. It is deterministic, not a model's mood.

Open: the exact bound values, which are business configuration and Level 3 research.
